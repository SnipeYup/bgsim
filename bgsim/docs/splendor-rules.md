# Splendor — rules

A game for 2 to 4 players. Players collect gem tokens and use them to buy
development cards. Cards give permanent gem bonuses that make later cards
cheaper, and some cards give prestige points. The first player to reach 15
prestige points triggers the end of the game.

## Components

- Gem tokens in five colours: white (diamond), blue (sapphire), green
  (emerald), red (ruby), black (onyx). Seven of each colour with 4 players,
  five of each with 3 players, four of each with 2 players.
- Five gold tokens (jokers). Always five, regardless of player count.
- 90 development cards in three levels: 40 level-1 cards, 30 level-2 cards,
  20 level-3 cards. Every card shows a cost (some number of tokens of one or
  more colours), a bonus (one of the five colours), and a prestige value
  (0 to 5 points).
- 10 noble tiles. Each is worth 3 prestige points and shows a requirement in
  bonuses: either 4 cards of each of two colours, or 3 cards of each of three
  colours.

## Setup

Shuffle each level of development cards separately into three face-down
decks. Reveal four cards from each deck face up in a row next to it. Shuffle
the noble tiles and reveal a number equal to the player count plus one; the
rest are returned to the box. Put out the gem and gold tokens as described
above.

## A turn

On your turn you must take exactly one of these four actions:

1. **Take three gem tokens of different colours.** You may take fewer than
   three if you choose, and you can only take from colours that have tokens
   available. You may never take gold this way.
2. **Take two gem tokens of the same colour.** Only allowed if that colour
   has at least four tokens available before you take them.
3. **Reserve a development card.** Take one face-up card from the table, or
   the top card of one of the three decks without looking at it, and put it
   in your hand. Also take one gold token, if any remain. You may hold at
   most three reserved cards; if you already hold three, you cannot reserve.
   Reserved cards are yours to buy later and cannot be taken by anyone else.
4. **Buy a development card.** Buy one face-up card from the table, or one of
   your reserved cards. Pay its cost in tokens. Every bonus card you own
   reduces the cost of that colour by one, permanently. Gold tokens count as
   any colour. Spent tokens return to the supply. The bought card goes face
   up in front of you; its bonus applies from now on and its prestige points
   count for you.

Whenever a face-up card is taken from the table (reserved or bought), it is
immediately replaced with the top card of the same deck. If that deck is
empty, the slot stays empty.

## End of turn

**Hand limit.** You may never end your turn with more than 10 tokens (gold
included). If you have more, return tokens of your choice to the supply until
you hold 10.

**Nobles.** After your action, check the face-up nobles. If your bonus cards
meet a noble's requirement, that noble comes to you: take the tile and score
its 3 points. If you qualify for more than one noble, you take only one this
turn and choose which. Nobles are not replaced. Bonus cards are not spent on
nobles; the same cards can qualify you for further nobles on later turns.
Only bonuses from cards count, never tokens.

## End of the game

When a player reaches 15 or more prestige points, the current round is
finished so that every player has taken the same number of turns (play
continues until the player to the right of the starting player has acted).
Then the game ends.

The player with the most prestige points wins. If tied, the tied player who
has bought the fewest development cards wins.
